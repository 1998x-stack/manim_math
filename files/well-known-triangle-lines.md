# Well-Known Lines in Triangle Geometry

## Encyclopedia of Triangle Centers Reference

This document lists the most important and well-known lines in triangle geometry, many of which are referenced in Clark Kimberling's Encyclopedia of Triangle Centers (ETC).

---

## Classical Lines

### 1. The Euler Line
- **Definition**: The line passing through the centroid (X(2)), circumcenter (X(3)), orthocenter (X(4)), and nine-point center (X(5))
- **Contains points**: X(2), X(3), X(4), X(5), X(20), X(21), X(22), X(23), and infinitely many others
- **Equation**: Centers X(2) through X(30), X(140), X(186), X(235), X(237), X(297), X(376)-X(379), X(381)-X(384), X(401)-X(475), and others lie on this line
- **Discovery**: Leonhard Euler (18th century)
- **Special property**: Does not exist for equilateral triangles (all centers coincide)
- **Ratio**: X(2) divides X(3)X(4) in ratio 1:2

### 2. Medians
- **Definition**: Lines from each vertex to the midpoint of the opposite side
- **Count**: Three medians (one from each vertex)
- **Concurrence point**: Centroid (X(2))
- **Property**: The centroid divides each median in ratio 2:1 from vertex
- **Construction**: 
  - Median from A to midpoint of BC
  - Median from B to midpoint of CA
  - Median from C to midpoint of AB

### 3. Angle Bisectors
- **Definition**: Lines that bisect each angle of the triangle
- **Count**: Three internal angle bisectors
- **Concurrence point**: Incenter (X(1))
- **Related**: Three external angle bisectors meet at the excenters
- **Property**: Each angle bisector divides the opposite side in ratio of adjacent sides

### 4. Altitudes
- **Definition**: Perpendicular lines from each vertex to the opposite side (or its extension)
- **Count**: Three altitudes
- **Concurrence point**: Orthocenter (X(4))
- **Construction**:
  - From A perpendicular to BC
  - From B perpendicular to CA
  - From C perpendicular to AB
- **Special case**: In a right triangle, the orthocenter is at the right-angle vertex

### 5. Perpendicular Bisectors (Mediators)
- **Definition**: Lines perpendicular to each side passing through its midpoint
- **Count**: Three perpendicular bisectors
- **Concurrence point**: Circumcenter (X(3))
- **Property**: Every point on a perpendicular bisector is equidistant from the endpoints
- **Related**: These lines form the boundaries of the Voronoi diagram of the vertices

### 6. Symmedians
- **Definition**: Reflections of the medians about the corresponding angle bisectors
- **Count**: Three symmedians
- **Concurrence point**: Symmedian point (X(6)), also known as Lemoine point
- **Construction**: 
  - Reflect median from A about the angle bisector at A
  - Repeat for B and C
- **Alternate definition**: Lines through vertices parallel to the tangent to the circumcircle at those vertices

## Advanced Classical Lines

### 7. Orthic Axis
- **Definition**: The line through the feet of the altitudes (when the triangle is acute)
- **Trilinear equation**: cos A : cos B : cos C
- **Property**: Perpendicular to the Euler line
- **Special case**: Exists only when the triangle is acute
- **Pole**: X(25) is the pole of the orthic axis with respect to the circumcircle

### 8. Gergonne Line
- **Definition**: Line connecting certain special points related to the Gergonne point
- **Related to**: Contact points of incircle with the sides
- **Contains**: Points related to the contact triangle

### 9. Nagel Line  
- **Definition**: Line related to the Nagel point and excentral triangle
- **Related to**: Semiperimeter constructions

### 10. Line at Infinity
- **Definition**: The ideal line where parallel lines meet in projective geometry
- **Intersection with Euler Line**: X(30) = Euler Infinity Point
- **Property**: In projective geometry, this completes the plane
- **Contains**: All ideal points (directions)

## Lines Related to Specific Constructions

### 11. Antiorthic Axis
- **Related to**: The antiorthic triangle (formed by reflections of vertices about opposite sides)
- **Property**: Analogous to orthic axis but for the antiorthic triangle

### 12. Brocard Axis
- **Definition**: The line connecting the circumcenter (X(3)) and symmedian point (X(6))
- **Contains**: First and second Brocard points
- **Property**: Important in Brocard geometry
- **Special property**: Perpendicular to line X(3)X(76) at X(3)

### 13. Fermat Axis
- **Related to**: The Fermat (Torricelli) points X(13) and X(14)
- **Construction**: Involves equilateral triangles erected on the sides

### 14. Steiner Line
- **Related to**: The Steiner point and Steiner ellipse
- **Property**: Connected to the inscribed ellipse of maximum area

### 15. Soddy Line
- **Related to**: Centers of Soddy circles (circles tangent to all three excircles)
- **Contains**: Inner and outer Soddy centers

## Special Line Configurations

### 21. Cevian Lines
- **Definition**: Lines from vertices through a common point (the Cevian point)
- **Creates**: A Cevian triangle (formed by the intersection points with opposite sides)
- **Example**: Medians are Cevian lines for the centroid
- **Property**: Three Cevian lines always form a Cevian triangle

### 22. Isogonal Conjugate Lines
- **Definition**: Pairs of lines reflected about angle bisectors
- **Property**: Isogonal conjugates of concurrent lines are also concurrent
- **Example**: Altitudes and lines to circumcenter are isogonal conjugates

### 23. Isotomic Conjugate Lines
- **Definition**: Lines related by reflection about medians
- **Property**: Parallel to trilinear polar lines

### 24. Simson Line (Wallace Line)
- **Definition**: For a point P on the circumcircle, the feet of perpendiculars from P to the three sides are collinear
- **Property**: This line is called the Simson line of point P
- **Special case**: The Simson line rotates as P moves around the circumcircle
- **Named after**: Robert Simson (though actually discovered by William Wallace)

## Lines Related to Special Circles

### 25. Nine-Point Circle Tangent Line
- **Point of tangency**: Feuerbach point (X(11))
- **Property**: The nine-point circle is tangent to the incircle here
- **Related**: Also tangent to the three excircles

### 26. Incircle Tangent Lines
- **Count**: Three lines (tangent at three points on the sides)
- **Property**: These three points of tangency form the contact triangle
- **Related**: Connect to Gergonne point

### 27. Circumcircle Tangent Lines
- **Count**: Three tangent lines (one at each vertex)
- **Property**: Form the excentral triangle when extended
- **Related**: Symmedians are parallel to these at vertices

---

## Trilinear Polar Lines

### 28. Trilinear Polar of a Point
- **Definition**: For a point P with trilinears p:q:r, the trilinear polar has equation
  px + qy + rz = 0
- **Property**: The locus of points whose Cevian triangle is in perspective with ABC
- **Examples**:
  - Trilinear polar of X(1) relates to the line at infinity
  - Trilinear polar of various centers creates important lines

### 29. Antiorthic Polar
- **Related to**: Antiorthic triangle and its properties
- **Construction**: Analogous to trilinear polar

---

## Parallel Lines

### 30. Lines Parallel to Sides
- **Through Centroid**: Lines parallel to each side through the centroid divide the triangle into specific ratios
- **Through Other Centers**: Various centers create specific parallel line configurations

### 31. Lines Parallel to Euler Line
- **Count**: Infinitely many
- **Special cases**: Lines through specific triangle centers parallel to Euler line
- **Property**: The line at infinity includes the Euler infinity point X(30)

---

## Perspectivity Lines

### 32. Perspectrix (Axis of Perspectivity)
- **Definition**: When two triangles are in perspective from a point (perspector), their corresponding sides meet at points on a line (the perspectrix)
- **Example**: ABC and its medial triangle are in perspective
- **Related**: Desargues' theorem

### 33. Homothetic Axes
- **Definition**: Lines of homothety between triangles
- **Property**: Centers of homothety lie on these axes

## Construction Methods for Lines

### 1. Concurrence Method
- Draw three lines that concur at a triangle center
- Examples: medians, altitudes, angle bisectors

### 2. Locus Method
- Find the locus of points satisfying a condition
- Example: Simson line as locus of feet of perpendiculars

### 3. Polar Method
- Use trilinear or barycentric polars
- Creates lines from points algebraically

### 4. Reflection Method
- Reflect known lines about other lines
- Example: Symmedians from medians

### 5. Perspectivity Method
- Find lines through perspectors of triangle pairs
- Uses Desargues' theorem

## Properties of Lines in Triangle Geometry

### Concurrency
- Three or more lines meeting at a single point
- Fundamental to triangle center theory

### Collinearity  
- Three or more points lying on a single line
- Creates important relationships between centers

### Parallelism
- Lines that never meet (except at infinity)
- Important for homothety and similarity

### Perpendicularity
- Lines meeting at right angles
- Creates orthogonal relationships

### Harmonic Division
- Lines dividing segments in specific ratios
- Important in projective geometry

## Historical Development

- **Ancient Greeks** (Euclid, etc.): Medians, angle bisectors, altitudes, perpendicular bisectors
- **Euler** (18th century): Euler line discovery
- **19th Century**: Simson line, symmedians, Brocard axis, Lemoine geometry
- **20th-21st Century**: Thousands of new lines catalogued in ETC
