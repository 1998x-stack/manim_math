# Well-Known Triangle Centers (Points)

## Encyclopedia of Triangle Centers Reference

This document lists the most well-known and classical triangle centers from Clark Kimberling's Encyclopedia of Triangle Centers (ETC).

---

## Classical Triangle Centers (The First 10 Points)

### X(1) - Incenter
- **Definition**: The point of concurrence of the interior angle bisectors of triangle ABC
- **Trilinears**: 1 : 1 : 1
- **Barycentrics**: a : b : c
- **Property**: The point inside ABC whose distances from sidelines BC, CA, AB are equal
- **Related**: Center of the incircle with radius r = 2×area(ABC)/(a + b + c)
- **Also known as**: The identity element of triangle centers under trilinear multiplication

### X(2) - Centroid
- **Definition**: The point of concurrence of the medians of ABC
- **Trilinears**: 1/a : 1/b : 1/c = bc : ca : ab
- **Barycentrics**: 1 : 1 : 1
- **Property**: Situated 1/3 of the distance from each vertex to the midpoint of the opposite side
- **Physical meaning**: The center of mass; a triangular sheet balances atop a pin at this point
- **Also known as**: Center of gravity, the identity element under barycentric multiplication

### X(3) - Circumcenter
- **Definition**: The point of concurrence of the perpendicular bisectors of the sides
- **Trilinears**: cos A : cos B : cos C
- **Barycentrics**: sin 2A : sin 2B : sin 2C
- **Property**: Equidistant from all three vertices A, B, C
- **Related**: Center of the circumcircle with radius R = a/(2 sin A) = abc/(4×area(ABC))

### X(4) - Orthocenter
- **Definition**: The point of concurrence of the altitudes of ABC
- **Trilinears**: sec A : sec B : sec C
- **Barycentrics**: tan A : tan B : tan C
- **Property**: Forms an orthocentric system with the three vertices
- **Note**: Each of the four points (A, B, C, X(4)) is the orthocenter of the triangle formed by the other three

### X(5) - Nine-Point Center
- **Definition**: The center of the nine-point circle
- **Trilinears**: cos(B - C) : cos(C - A) : cos(A - B)
- **Property**: The nine-point circle passes through:
  - The three midpoints of the sides
  - The three feet of the altitudes
  - The three midpoints of segments from vertices to the orthocenter
- **Special**: The radius of the nine-point circle is one-half the circumradius

### X(6) - Symmedian Point
- **Definition**: The point of concurrence of the symmedians
- **Trilinears**: a : b : c = sin A : sin B : sin C
- **Barycentrics**: a² : b² : c²
- **Construction**: Reflections of medians about corresponding angle bisectors
- **Also known as**: Lemoine Point, Grebe Point
- **Property**: Minimizes the sum of squares of trilinear distances: x² + y² + z²
- **Special**: The centroid of its own pedal triangle

### X(7) - Gergonne Point
- **Definition**: Point of concurrence of lines from vertices to opposite contact points of incircle
- **Trilinears**: bc/(b + c - a) : ca/(c + a - b) : ab/(a + b - c)
- **Barycentrics**: 1/(b + c - a) : 1/(c + a - b) : 1/(a + b - c)
- **Construction**: Let A', B', C' be the points where the incircle touches BC, CA, AB respectively; then AA', BB', CC' concur at X(7)

### X(8) - Nagel Point
- **Definition**: Point of concurrence of lines from vertices to opposite semiperimeter points
- **Trilinears**: (b + c - a)/a : (c + a - b)/b : (a + b - c)/c
- **Barycentrics**: b + c - a : c + a - b : a + b - c
- **Construction**: Trace half the perimeter from each vertex around the triangle
- **Also known as**: Incenter of the anticomplementary triangle

### X(9) - Mittenpunkt
- **Definition**: The symmedian point of the excentral triangle
- **Trilinears**: b + c - a : c + a - b : a + b - c
- **Barycentrics**: a(b + c - a) : b(c + a - b) : c(a + b - c)
- **Also equals**: cot(A/2) : cot(B/2) : cot(C/2)

### X(10) - Spieker Center
- **Definition**: The center of the Spieker circle (incircle of the medial triangle)
- **Trilinears**: bc(b + c) : ca(c + a) : ab(a + b)
- **Barycentrics**: b + c : c + a : a + b
- **Property**: The centroid of the perimeter of triangle ABC

## Other Well-Known Centers

### X(11) - Feuerbach Point
- **Definition**: The point of tangency of the nine-point circle and the incircle
- **Property**: Feuerbach's theorem states that the nine-point circle is tangent to the incircle and the three excircles
- **Named after**: Karl Wilhelm Feuerbach (1800-1834)

### X(13) - First Isogonic Center (Fermat Point)
- **Definition**: Constructed using equilateral triangles on the sides (negative side)
- **Property**: If all angles of ABC are < 2π/3, this point minimizes the sum |AX| + |BX| + |CX|
- **Also known as**: Torricelli Point
- **Related**: The angles BXC, CXA, AXB are all equal to 120°

### X(14) - Second Isogonic Center
- **Definition**: Constructed using equilateral triangles on the sides (positive side)
- **Property**: The antipodal isogonic center to X(13)

### X(15) - First Isodynamic Point
- **Trilinears**: sin(A + π/3) : sin(B + π/3) : sin(C + π/3)
- **Property**: The pedal triangle is equilateral
- **Related**: Lies on the Apollonian circles

### X(16) - Second Isodynamic Point
- **Trilinears**: sin(A - π/3) : sin(B - π/3) : sin(C - π/3)
- **Property**: The pedal triangle is equilateral

### X(17) - First Napoleon Point
- **Definition**: Center of the equilateral triangle constructed outward on side BC, and cyclically
- **Related**: Napoleon's theorem about equilateral triangles

### X(18) - Second Napoleon Point
- **Definition**: Center of the equilateral triangle constructed inward on side BC, and cyclically

### X(19) - Clawson Point
- **Trilinears**: tan A : tan B : tan C
- **Barycentrics**: a tan A : b tan B : c tan C
- **Property**: Homothetic center of the orthic and extangents triangles

### X(20) - de Longchamps Point
- **Definition**: The reflection of the orthocenter (X(4)) about the circumcenter (X(3))
- **Property**: The orthocenter of the anticomplementary triangle
- **Barycentrics**: tan B + tan C - tan A : tan C + tan A - tan B : tan A + tan B - tan C

### X(21) - Schiffler Point
- **Definition**: Concurrence point of Euler lines of triangles IBC, ICA, IAB, and ABC (where I = incenter)
- **Trilinears**: 1/(cos B + cos C) : 1/(cos C + cos A) : 1/(cos A + cos B)

### X(22) - Exeter Point
- **Definition**: The perspector of the circummedial triangle and the tangential triangle
- **Barycentrics**: a²(b⁴ + c⁴ - a⁴) : b²(c⁴ + a⁴ - b⁴) : c²(a⁴ + b⁴ - c⁴)

### X(23) - Far-Out Point
- **Definition**: The inverse of the centroid in the circumcircle
- **Property**: Related to the Brocard circle and advanced triangle geometry

### X(24) - Perspector of ABC and Orthic-of-Orthic Triangle
- **Trilinears**: sec A cos 2A : sec B cos 2B : sec C cos 2C

### X(25) - Homothetic Center of Orthic and Tangential Triangles
- **Trilinears**: sin A tan A : sin B tan B : sin C tan C
- **Barycentrics**: a²/(b² + c² - a²) : b²/(c² + a² - b²) : c²(a² + b² - c²)
- **Property**: Pole of the orthic axis with respect to the circumcircle

### X(39) - Brocard Midpoint
- **Definition**: The midpoint between the first and second Brocard points
- **Trilinears**: a(b² + c²) : b(c² + a²) : c(a² + b²)
- **Property**: Important in Brocard geometry and the Brocard circle

### X(40) - Reflection of Incenter in Circumcenter
- **Definition**: The point where perpendiculars from excenters to respective sides concur
- **Property**: Also the circumcenter of the excentral triangle

## Special Categories of Points

### Brocard Points (Not Triangle Centers)
- **First Brocard Point**: Trilinears c/b : a/c : b/a
- **Second Brocard Point**: Trilinears b/c : c/a : a/b
- **Note**: These are NOT triangle centers because they are not invariant under reflection
- **Property**: Form a bicentric pair; lie on the Brocard circle

### Fermat Points
- Related to X(13) and X(14)
- Minimize or relate to the sum of distances to vertices
- Connected to equilateral triangle constructions

## Historical Notes

- **Ancient Greeks**: Knew the centroid (X(2)), incenter (X(1)), circumcenter (X(3)), and orthocenter (X(4))
- **Euler**: Proved that X(2), X(3), X(4), and X(5) are collinear (Euler line)
- **19th Century**: Discovery of symmedian point, Brocard points, and many others
- **Modern Era**: Clark Kimberling's Encyclopedia (started 1998) now catalogs over 70,000+ centers

## Key Properties and Relationships

### Euler Line
- Contains X(2), X(3), X(4), X(5), X(20), and infinitely many other centers
- One of the most important lines in triangle geometry

### Orthocentric System
- The four points {A, B, C, X(4)} form an orthocentric system
- Each is the orthocenter of the triangle formed by the other three

### Nine-Point Circle
- Passes through 9 specific points of the triangle
- Has radius R/2 (half the circumradius)
- Tangent to incircle and all three excircles (Feuerbach's theorem)

## Coordinate Systems

### Trilinear Coordinates
- Expressed as x : y : z
- Represent signed distances from point to sidelines
- Natural for angle-based definitions

### Barycentric Coordinates  
- Expressed as u : v : w
- Represent mass distribution or signed areas
- If trilinears are x : y : z, then barycentrics are ax : by : cz
